Rational Replacements: the sin, cos and tan Audit

Section 12.1 of the operator specification lists four algebraic replacements for direct implementation. A rotation, a sine ratio and a hyperbolic distance can each be computed with no sin, cos, tan or square root, from rationals alone. This page verifies each equation exactly and live, and audits every transcendental call in Hyperbolic Oracles and the Geometric Calculator against them. It also logs what changed: the calculator keypad’s sin and cos were 3-term Taylor polynomials, and the exact Cayley map now uses its closed form.

Where this sits

The replacements are a shared kernel. The ghost map and the exact core feed it, and it feeds the PolyStep exact track (through the Cayley map), the 0.7 Claude fence (through the ghost kernel) and the Geometric Calculator’s keypad.

03

Eq. 85: sin, cos and tan from a quadrance

85  For \(\mathbf u = \tan(\theta/2)\hat{\mathbf u} \in \mathbb Q^3\) with \(Q = \|\mathbf u\|^2\): \(\displaystyle \cos\theta = \frac{1 - Q}{1 + Q},\ \ \sin^2\theta = \frac{4Q}{(1+Q)^2},\ \ \tan^2\theta = \frac{4Q}{(1-Q)^2}.\)

With \(t = \tan(\theta/2)\), \(Q = t^2\). Then \(\cos\theta = \cos^2\tfrac\theta2 - \sin^2\tfrac\theta2 = \frac{1 - t^2}{1 + t^2}\) and \(\sin\theta = \frac{2t}{1+t^2}\). Squaring and dividing gives the other two. \(\tan^2\) has its pole at \(Q = 1\), \(\theta = \pi/2\). The point \(\big(\frac{1-t^2}{1+t^2}, \frac{2t}{1+t^2}\big)\) is rational and lies exactly on the unit circle.

The keypad. A rational angle \(a\) has no rational sine (Niven), so the calculator must approximate somewhere. The old keypad approximated the functions themselves, with \(a - a^3/6 + a^5/120\) and \(1 - a^2/2 + a^4/24\). The new one approximates only the parameter. It reduces \(a\) modulo \(2\pi\) (64-digit \(\pi\)), finds \(t = \tan(r/4)\) to \(10^{-60}\), keeps it as a rational, and from there uses eq. 85 and the double angle exactly. The answer is an exact point on the circle, and every printed digit is right.

04

Eq. 86: rotations without square roots

86  \(\displaystyle \mathrm{Rot}(\mathbf u)\mathbf x = \mathbf x + \frac{2}{1 + Q}\big(\mathbf u \times \mathbf x + \mathbf u \times (\mathbf u \times \mathbf x)\big)\) is the rotation by \(\theta\) about \(\hat{\mathbf u}\), and is rational for rational \(\mathbf u\) and \(\mathbf x\).

Rodrigues: \(R = I + \sin\theta\,K + (1 - \cos\theta)K^2\) with \(K = [\hat{\mathbf u}]_\times\). With \(\mathbf u = t\hat{\mathbf u}\), \(\sin\theta\,K = \frac{2t}{1+t^2}K = \frac{2}{1+Q}[\mathbf u]_\times\) and \((1 - \cos\theta)K^2 = \frac{2t^2}{1+t^2}K^2 = \frac{2}{1+Q}[\mathbf u]_\times^2\). The Cayley map is the same rotation. For a skew \(A\) in two or three dimensions, \(A^3 = -QA\), where \(Q\) is the sum of the squared entries above the diagonal. With \(k = \frac{2}{1+Q}\): \((I + A)\big(I + k(A^2 - A)\big) = I + A + k(A^3 - A) = I + A - k(1 + Q)A = I - A\). Since \(I + A\) and \(I - A\) commute, \((I - A)(I + A)^{-1} = I + \frac{2(A^2 - A)}{1 + Q}\), with no inverse. The live check R2 confirms it against the inverse, Fraction for Fraction. With \(A = [\mathbf u]_\times\) this is \(\mathrm{Rot}(-\mathbf u)\). So every Cayley rotation in Hyperbolic PolyStep since Phase 1 was eq. 86; only its evaluation changed.

Eq. 87: sine ratios without sines

87  For a vertex \(A\) over the base \(B = 0\), \(C = 2C_0\), with \(Q_3 = \|A - B\|^2\) and \(Q_2 = \|A - C\|^2\): the internal symmedian foot is \(x_F = \frac{Q_3}{Q_2 + Q_3}2C_0\), the external Apollonius point is \(x_E = \frac{Q_3}{Q_3 - Q_2}2C_0\), and \((0, 2C_0; x_E, x_F) = -1\).

The symmedian from \(A\) is the median reflected in the angle bisector. By the ratio lemma it divides \(BC\) as \(BF/FC = (AB/AC)^2\cdot\frac{\sin}{\sin}\) of the reflected angles, which cancel, leaving \(Q_3/Q_2\). So \(x_F = \frac{Q_3}{Q_2+Q_3}\cdot 2C_0\). The external division in the same ratio gives \(x_E\). Internal and external division in one ratio are harmonic: \(\frac{x_E - 0}{x_E - 2C_0} \Big/ \frac{x_F - 0}{x_F - 2C_0} = \frac{-Q_3/Q_2}{Q_3/Q_2} = -1\). An isosceles triangle (\(Q_2 = Q_3\)) has its Apollonius point at infinity.

Eq. 88: the ghost kernel, and the drift sum

88a  For \(\mathbf p, \mathbf r\) in the Poincaré ball, \(\displaystyle Q_h(\mathbf p, \mathbf r) = \Big(\frac{(1+|\mathbf p|^2)(1+|\mathbf r|^2) - 4\mathbf p\cdot\mathbf r}{(1-|\mathbf p|^2)(1-|\mathbf r|^2)}\Big)^2 - 1 = \sinh^2 d(\mathbf p, \mathbf r).\)

The ghost map lifts \(\mathbf p\) to \(G(\mathbf p) = \big(1 + |\mathbf p|^2, 2\mathbf p\big)/(1 - |\mathbf p|^2)\) on the hyperboloid. Then \(-\langle G(\mathbf p), G(\mathbf r)\rangle = \frac{(1+|\mathbf p|^2)(1+|\mathbf r|^2) - 4\mathbf p\cdot\mathbf r}{(1-|\mathbf p|^2)(1-|\mathbf r|^2)} = \cosh d\), and \(\cosh^2 - 1 = \sinh^2\). The 0.7 Claude fence decides with this quantity. Computed from its centre as \(w_i = (b_i^2 - \sigma)/\sigma\), it is exact, and arcosh is never needed for a verdict.

88b  \(\varepsilon_{\text{net}} = \varepsilon_1 + \varepsilon_2 + \varepsilon_3 + \varepsilon_1\times\varepsilon_2 + \varepsilon_1\times\varepsilon_3 + \varepsilon_2\times\varepsilon_3\) is the Gibbs vector of \(R(\varepsilon_1)R(\varepsilon_2)R(\varepsilon_3)\) to second order. It is not exact, and it holds in that order only.

Gibbs vectors compose exactly by \(R(a)R(b) = R(c)\), \(c = \frac{a + b + a\times b}{1 - a\cdot b}\). This is checked against the product of the matrices of eq. 86, Fraction for Fraction. Expanding, \(c = a + b + a\times b + O(3)\). Composing a third, \(R(c)R(e_3)\) adds \(e_3 + (e_1 + e_2)\times e_3\). That gives the stated sum with error \(O(\varepsilon^3)\). In the opposite order \(R(\varepsilon_3)R(\varepsilon_2)R(\varepsilon_1)\) every cross product changes sign, so the stated sum is off by \(2\sum_{i<j}\varepsilon_i\times\varepsilon_j = O(\varepsilon^2)\). If the \(\varepsilon_i\) are rotation vectors (angle × axis) rather than tangent half-angle vectors, BCH gives a cross coefficient of \(\tfrac12\), not 1.

What was kept, and why

  • Inputs converted once (input). A configured step radius, or a measured rotation in degrees, is real. It is turned into a rational exactly once: the step’s \(t = \tanh(r/2)\), or the engines’ Gibbs vector \(u = v/(1 + w)\) in exactLeg. Everything after that is eq. 85 or 86.
  • Terminals marked approximate (labelled). The Zero-Point and Convergence terminals return floats for sin and cos and say so. They could adopt the keypad’s kernel; nothing in them depends on it.
  • The float search track (float). Hyperbolic PolyStep’s track A uses cosh, sinh and arcosh. Its bits are fixed by the reference traces and by 7,740 preregistered runs, so it is not rewritten. Logged as a candidate for a re-registered round: the horizon test \(\operatorname{arcosh} x_0 > R\) as \(x_0 > \cosh R\), with no arcosh per point.
  • Intrinsically transcendental (intrinsic). Softmax and Sinkhorn weights, random normals, the cosine schedule and the fence’s portable exp/log kernels have no rational form, and none is wanted.
  • The engines’ float model (model) of the toroidal dynamics, and drawing (display): screen coordinates and animation never feed back into exact arithmetic.
