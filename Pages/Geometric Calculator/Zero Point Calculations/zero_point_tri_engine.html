Zero-Point Tri-Engine Zero Point Calculations · the eight-step cycle inside the 720-frame register

The Tri-Engine Convergence calculator, rebuilt on the zero-point cycle of The Geometric Engine of Time: The Zero-Point Eight-Step Cycle. Every frame of the 720-frame outer gear is one zero-point step: spinor step k = F mod 8, branch j = F mod 3, joint frame n = F mod 24. So one register holds 90 spinor cycles and 30 joint cycles (Proposition 3.7), not one stretched cycle. The three engines are the three 120° branches. Each carries one instruction of its calculation per joint cycle: onloaded at its k = 2 frame, lifted, projected, inverted and folded back exactly, then offloaded at its k = 6 frame twelve frames later. Calculations of different lengths finish at different times. The three results pair at the zero point, and every register closes with the 720 → 72 → 10 pump and the open memory test. Telemetry log

  • What changes from the convergence calculator. There, the eight-step cycle was read off the outer gear as one step per 90 frames, so one 720-frame turn was one spinor cycle. The paper supersedes that reading (Errata 10.2, item 2): 720 is a frame count, and under the exact generator 1 + i the spinor returns every 8 frames, 90 times per register (Proposition 3.7(4)). Here every frame is a zero-point step, and everything that was gated by the old windows is now gated by the frame schedule.
  • Branches are engines. The nine-phase wheel splits into three 120° triads (Proposition 2.1). The a = 0 triad, in the corrected frame, is three cube vertices, and σ, the 120° turn that relates the three engines, carries each branch to the next. So engine s is branch s. A branch onloads at its k = 2 frame and offloads 12 frames later at k = 6 (Proposition 3.7), once per joint cycle. A calculation therefore runs one instruction per joint cycle, and calculations of different lengths finish at different frames.
  • Pairing at the zero point. When all three have latched, they pair at the next zero point of the joint cycle (joint frame 0, a Snap). Their exact total is the fourth of the triple quad, the C₃ split gives s and z, and the landing triangle is read out by the TQF. The zero point is a closure state with three tests (Definition 3.12). The spinor test is the clock itself. The geometric test is every packet's exact round trip. The memory test is the open holonomy of each engine's gear over the register, which the 720 → 72 → 10 pump evaluates (Proposition 3.9). With the clutch holding a finished engine, that engine's gear falls behind and the register closes with a recorded residual. That is the case Definition 3.12 allows for: a residual P ≠ 1 that a compensator (H = P⁻¹) removes.
  • Four tracks. The spinor clock is computed three ways (§8.10). Floating point (track A) drifts slowly. The exact reduced state (track B) cannot drift. Residues modulo 881 (track R) fingerprint B. The page accepts A only within tolerance of B.
  • Frame convention. This page keeps engine v10's frame, 12 per pulse, so the register is one second. The paper takes a frame to be one pulse of 1/60 s, so its register is 12 s. Its zero-point identities (Propositions 3.7, 3.9, 7.1) count frames and hold under either convention. Its clock-window table (Proposition 3.8) is stated for its own frame and is not used here DEFINITION.
  • What it does not claim. The lifts into the null cone and the hyperboloid are embeddings that change how a point is represented (Definition 1.1). The value of each calculation comes from the exact evaluation, and nothing here bears on P versus NP, which the paper leaves open (Theorem 5.11).
