00

What this is

In Owl Academy, STRIX is a familiar that remembers a conversation as engrams: each word becomes a 64-bit grid, the last three sit in a window, and every word is logged as a 128-bit packet. The Mind’s Eye draws that memory as a lattice. Its quaternion side, the S.P.I.R.E. layer, runs in floating point and is spread over three places, none of them exact.

This system keeps the engram key exactly as Owl Academy defines it and rebuilds everything above it on code that is already exact here. A word runs through the five tiers of the one-engine calculator. The window runs on the three-engine clock. The memory is a triple quad with a Merkle chain that finds and repairs an altered cell. The soliton gauge reads every word’s place in the window. Hyperbolic PolyStep gives every word an exact quaternion address and lays the memory out in H² and H³. The fence watches the layout.

What it rebuilds

Owl Academy’s Engram Mind Eye, Engram Codec and Biomesh Mind’s Eye 3D: STRIX, the packet chain, the quaternion offload and the lattice.

How

Eight layers, from the frozen key to the watch layer, each built from a system this site already has. The architecture explainer walks one word through all of them.

Why it should work

Four feasibility spikes on the two repositories’ own code, and a golden corpus taken from Owl Academy’s files, before any engram code is written.
What it will not do

Exact here means exact bookkeeping: addresses, chains, memory words, registers, frames and gauges. It does not make recall understand meaning. The engram/1 key records only whether each letter appears an odd or even number of times, so LISTEN and SILENT share a key, and no geometry placed on top of it adds meaning. The fence finds misplaced memories, not new ideas. Claim E9 states this in advance.

Glossary

Engram
A 64-bit grid, 8 × 8, made by XOR-ing a grid per letter of a word. engram/1 is Owl Academy’s definition, frozen here.
STRIX
Owl Academy’s familiar: it remembers a conversation as engrams and answers by recalling the nearest one.
Window α, β, γ
The last three engrams. Here they ride the three engines of the zero-point clock.
Y
The parity cell, α ⊕ β ⊕ γ. With it any one lost cell is rebuilt.
Packet
One word’s record. In Owl Academy, 128 bits: codex parity, the grid, and its distances to the window. Here also its exact address and two Einstein tiles.
Address
A 28-letter reduced word in 1 ± 2i, 1 ± 2j, 1 ± 2k: one integer quaternion that holds a packet’s 64 bits exactly.
Chain quaternion
The product of a chain’s addresses in order. Right division unfolds it back into its packets.
Einstein tile
Engine v10’s 64-bit word: halo (step, wheel, structure), gate, control (form, slot, scale) and a 32-bit core.
Register
720 frames. Its packets telescope through 72 blocks and 10 groups, as in the 720 → 72 → 10 pump.
Gauge
Proposition 7.14’s exact, invertible reading of three values as their total and their lean. Equal values lock.
Free-run + membrane
The Zero-Point Tri-Engine’s clutch setting in which every engine turns on every frame and the membrane removes the differential at each zero point.
Fence, guard
The 0.7 Claude fence flags points too far from the centre; the guard proves a stored distance wrong when four points break the ln 2 bound.
