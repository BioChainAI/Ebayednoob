1. The Plain Au Layer (Low-Q Cavity)

According to Simone (2021), achieving strong coupling between molecules and light usually requires high-quality microcavities.

Here we see a simulated Plain Au (Gold) Layer. In classical physics, this is considered a terrible substrate for coupling. Its Quality Factor ($Q$) is extremely low:

$$ Q = \frac{\omega_{SPP}}{\kappa} \approx 0.3 $$

Because $Q$ is so small, energy leaks rapidly. Any standard resonant wave placed here will quickly decay into thermal noise.
