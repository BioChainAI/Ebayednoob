01

Where this sits

The benchmark ladder’s Stage 2 is lexical semantics: what people judge about pairs of words. Stage 1 asked whether the exact trees are faithful copies of WordNet. Stage 2 asks whether that copy tracks human judgements, and where a flat construction or a corpus does better. This page’s focus node is Stage 2’s lexical claims.

The code is in reference/: the loaders and pins lexicon/stage2data.py, the sense rule senses.py, WordNet’s graph wngraph.py, the scores and their certified order scores2.py, the statistics and decision rules stats2.py, HiT’s rows hit.py, GloVe glove.py, and the runner run_claims_stage2.py. The browser’s re-evaluation is lexicon_stage2.js, beside the unedited lexicon_core.js, run by tests/parity-stage2.cjs.

Every number on this page is read from data/stage2_view_v1.js. make_stage2_view_v1.py writes it from the decisive record results/claims-stage2.json (summary claims-stage2.md), stage2-manifest.json, tuning-stage2.json, settings-stage2.json, the visible dry record results/dry/claims-stage2.json and the dry runs’ two sealed files, opened in step 8 and committed as gzip copies in results/dry/stage2-sealed/ (each checked against the SHA-256 that names it). Point at a number to see the file and the path it comes from. The page fills each number from that file when it loads, and the generator’s --check fails if the page as written differs. The rules are in PREREGISTRATION.md: Amendment 13 (the claims, items 1–21), 14 (the definitions after the dry run) and 15 (the frozen rates). Amendment 16, written with this page, records errata: the Node file’s name, one sentence of Amendment 14 on L14’s estimates, three readings the independent check made explicit, and a figure of Amendment 14 that the sealed file corrects. It changes no rule and no result. Where this page and those files differ, the files win.

How to read this page

supported and refuted mean what the registered rule says and nothing more. Each rule reads 95% bootstrap intervals of a difference between two arms. A prediction was registered with each claim. It is not part of the rule, and this page marks each one right, wrong or partly.

An interval is written as estimate [2.5%, 97.5%]. Decimals are rounded from the record’s values.

H3, H2
The exact trees: L3’s training-free points of every noun synset in H³ and H², with the origin o at the empty word’s point.
F3, F3*, F2, F2*
The same construction at curvature 0 (L5’s flat arms): the same words, placed in flat space. F collides on its lattice; F* shrinks each level.
WN
WordNet’s own hypernym graph, with no geometry.
G
The is-a score of a sense pair (s, t): r(s) − r(t) − d(s, t), with r the distance from o. It is at most 0, and exactly 0 when t lies on the path from o to s. A word pair takes the largest G over its senses (the maximum over senses).
Sym, Dep°
Sym is −d(s, t): it gives (x, y) and (y, x) one score. Dep° is the depth gap r(s) − r(t) of the senses G chose.
K3, S-full, W
Controls on H3’s points. K3: words from a content hash. S-fulli: the points shuffled among synsets. Wi: the senses shuffled among words of equal polysemy. Twenty of each; a control is read against its strongest permutation.
ρ
Spearman’s rank correlation with average ranks for ties, computed from exact integer sums.
Word-level interval
For a difference of ρ: it resamples words, not rows, because one word sits in many pairs. It is wider than a row bootstrap: in Amendment 14’s synthetic model (item 3) it was about 1.6–1.8 times the true spread. The row bootstrap is reported beside it and decides nothing.
AP, direction accuracy
AP: the average precision of the True rows when every row is ranked by G, with ties counted against the True row. Direction accuracy: the share of hypernym pairs with G(x, y) strictly above G(y, x).
RR
On HiT, the reciprocal rank of a child’s held-out ancestor among it and its 10 negatives, ties counted against it.
Cluster interval
For BLESS and WBLESS: it resamples the concepts the rows were built from.
02

What was asked

Four claims, registered by Amendment 13 on 2026-11-07 before any Stage-2 module existed. The Python reference decides all four.

  • L12, graded type-of. On HyperLex’s 2,163 noun pairs, rated 0–6 for “x is a type of y” and read by G: (a) H3 and H2 track the ratings better than F3, F3*, F2 and F2*; (b) G beats the depth gap Dep°, so the tree carries ancestry beyond generality; (c) G beats Sym, so it carries direction beyond similarity; (d) H3 tracks them as well as WordNet’s own graph, within ±0.02.
  • L13, detection and direction. On the hypernymy suite’s BLESS and WBLESS test folds, H3 and H2 beat curvature 0 in (a) BLESS detection AP, (b) WBLESS detection AP and (c) BLESS direction accuracy.
  • L14, learned geometry. Trained on WordNet’s 75,850 direct noun hypernym edges at d = 2 and 3, learned Lorentz embeddings beat learned Euclidean ones, both read by G: (a) on HiT’s multi-hop test, with random and with hard negatives; (b) on HyperLex’s nouns.
  • L15, similarity and relatedness. (a) Read by Sym, curvature 0 does as well as the exact trees on SimLex’s nouns and MEN’s noun pairs: the hyperbolic gain in ρ is at most 0.02. (b) GloVe’s 300-d cosine beats H3 on relatedness (MEN and WordSim’s relatedness pairs), and H3 beats GloVe on HyperLex.

Each claim has controls that must fail as required. If one does not, the parts it names are void, and a void part decides nothing. Apart from tuning’s scores on HiT’s validation split (chapter 6), no metric of any arm had been computed on any of these sets before the decisive run. One count was a partial preview: Amendment 13 counted, by HyperLex TYPE, which pairs are WordNet closure pairs before L12(d) was written. It says so (A13.20), and the record flags L12(d) as “registered after a partial preview (A13.20)”.

  • right ρ(H3) between 0.45 and 0.70: it is 0.624924.
  • right Every F arm at least 0.10 below ρ(H3): the smallest gap is 0.408. In F a bent path’s deficit grows with its legs and the lattice collides, so ancestors at one depth mix with non-ancestors at another.

Why (d) is refuted, and what that rests on

The interval lies entirely below the band [−0.02, +0.02], which is what refutes (d): its upper end, −0.112235, is 0.092235 below the band’s low end. Its lower end, −0.220516, passes that end by 0.200516 (the record’s failing excess). A chain of reported arms shows where the gap opens. Each step keeps G and the maximum over senses and changes one thing:

Step (reported)ρWhat changes

Most of the gap opens in the last step. That is where the embedding bends a path, and a spanning ancestor then loses a little G. Amendment 13 named this loss but did not size it.

The tie reading behind (d)

WN’s G takes only 13 distinct values on these pairs; H3’s takes 1,939. ρ is decided with average ranks for ties (A13.7). Breaking each arm’s ties against the ratings gives the tie-pessimistic ρ, and breaking them with the ratings the tie-optimistic one. WN’s range is 0.495551 to 0.933593; H3’s is 0.620763 to 0.628662. Under the pessimistic reading H3 would lead WN. A13.7 chose average ranks in advance, with this case named as the reason: a pessimistic reading would decide (d) by the arms’ tie counts. So this is not a deviation, but (d) does rest on it.

G’s known limits show too. Two words that share a synset score G = 0, the largest value. In H3 those pairs form the top class on their own, above every ancestor pair, whose G the embedding’s bends push below 0; in WN, where an ancestor also scores 0, they tie. Either way synonyms rank with or above the type-of pairs. Within the hyp strata, G runs against the ratings: H3’s ρ in hyp-1 to hyp-4 is −0.066406, −0.046884, −0.221226 and −0.188723 (reported), because every ancestor sits near 0.

L13 · Detection and direction on BLESS and WBLESS

The exact trees find hypernym pairs and their direction well. Their flat twins sit near chance, and F2 and F2* fall below it in places: F2’s BLESS AP is 0.091665 against a chance level of 0.096563, and F2* picks the right direction on 0.438023 of the pairs. Two controls that worked as designed

L14 control 2 needed its macro mean. Under a relabelling, every row whose positive is one synset shares that synset’s new point, and a few general synsets hold large shares of the rows. What the independent check made explicit (Amendment 16)

None changes a value or a decision. The first corrects a sentence of Amendment 14; the other two are conventions the registration leaves open, now stated.

  • L14’s estimates. Amendment 14 item 1 says an interval’s estimate is the exact difference evaluated at 60 digits and rounded once to binary64. That holds for the ρ, AP and accuracy differences. For the intervals over HiT’s rows (L14(a) and the RR cells of control 3), the record reports rank.ci_mean’s own estimate instead: the float64 mean of the per-row differences, each RR rounded first, as A13.7 registered (“rank.ci_mean, unchanged”). The two differ in the last bit or two. Amendment 16 corrects Amendment 14’s sentence.
  • L13’s AP intervals. A resampled AP sums its terms in float64. The record sums them with numpy’s pairwise sum; a correctly rounded sum moves a few interval ends by a bit or two, none at six decimals. A13.7 says only “in float64”.
  • The tie bounds of ρ (reported) break every tie of the arm, including rows tied in both the arm and the ratings.

12

What this does not show

Amendment 13 item 21 listed what none of this decides. The results add a few limits of their own.

  • Discovery, or knowledge beyond WordNet. Every arm reads WordNet, and HyperLex’s TYPE, most of BLESS’s positives and all of HiT were built from it. L12–L14 say what curvature and learning do with the same information. L15(b) compares two sources of information, not two geometries.
  • Senses in context, verbs and adjectives, words outside WordNet. The decided sets are nouns. Verbs appear only in the dry run.
  • Learned geometry beyond this setting. Tables trained on the closure or all nouns, d ≥ 5, the instance-only synsets, other trainers, and readings from a base point other than the origin. The rates sit at the grid’s top, and where the scores would peak is unknown.
  • Comparison with published numbers. Subsets, WordNet versions, folds, rules for missing words and trainers all differ. Every literature number here is orientation.
  • The sets not decided: SemEval-2018 Task 9, HiT’s mixed-hop split, LEDS, EVALution, Shwartz and BiBLESS (the last four reported only).
  • Whether hyperbolic geometry helps a language model. That is for Stages 3 and 4, by amendment.
  • Exactness. G is ordered by square roots certified at 120 and 240 digits, not computed in rationals. Values within the 240-digit bound count as ties; that rule is part of the metric (A13.6).
  • One run, one machine. One decisive run in one container. The word-level intervals are conservative (Amendment 14 item 3); L12(d) and L15(a) are refuted even so.
  • L12(d) rests on average ranks. A13.7 registered them, and the reason; under tie-pessimistic ρ the order of H3 and WN would flip (chapter 4).
  • L13 compares H with near-chance flat arms. The large gaps describe F as much as H.
  • The browser check covers A13.14’s decision files. The Node re-evaluation reproduced all 106: the exact-arm files the decided comparisons read and the measured tables’ files. Files that only controls read were not re-rendered in Node: L14’s edge-shuffled tables (compared in control 3), its tuning tables and relabelled points (controls 1 and 2), and GloVe’s files for the controls 3 of L13 and L15. Amendment 16 item 1 lists every file not rendered. The independent check reproduced these with its own Python code.
