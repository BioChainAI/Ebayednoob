The Shared Loom

All three simulations below are woven from the same underlying geometry: a set of 5 nested toroidal "tiers" (T0 through T4), each governed by the same three winding laws.

Rn = 5n² + 15n + 15  (major radius)  •  rn = 2n + 4  (tube radius)  •  Wn = 16 − 2n  (helical winding number)

Each tier is rotated 90° relative to its neighbor (alternating X and Y axes) so the tiers nest without intersecting, and each carries 6 helical channels — 3 "Forward" (orthodromic, +1 chirality) and 3 "Inverse" (antidromic, −1 chirality) — wound around it in a dual-helix. That's the "loom": a fixed geometric scaffold. What changes between the three modules below is what gets woven through it — a self-verifying clock, a cavity-coupling simulation, or a holographic memory trace.

Framed loosely as physics (clockwork, cavity QED, holographic storage), these are geometric art/exploration tools rather than physical models — treat the terminology as metaphor for the underlying math, not a claim about real hardware.

1. The Geometric Engine of Time

5-Tier Clockwork

The base module. Time is parameterized by spatial progression around the 5 tiers instead of an external frame rate — the 60 total helical wraps across all tiers (WΣ = ∑(16 − 2n) = 60) act as the clock's intrinsic register. A single Master Clock Frequency slider drives every tier's rotation at once, with kinematic acceleration increasing toward the innermost T0 core.

The interesting part is the TQF (Triple Quad Formula) Area-Stability panel: it tracks the 3 forward anchor points on the T0 core in real time, computes their pairwise squared distances (quadrances Q1, Q2, Q3), and checks a rational-trigonometry identity (AR ≡ 16A², equivalent to Heron's formula) against the live geometry every frame — a continuous, visible proof that the winding stays topologically consistent as it spins.

T2 Equatorial Canvas

Isolates a single tier — the equatorial T2 manifold (W2 = 12 helical wraps, the balance point of the 5-tier stack) — and turns it into a recording surface.
