01

Where this sits

Stage F of the front end is a learned table: one point per synset, trained so that a synset’s relatives sit near it. Explainer 5 set it up as baseline B1, learned static hyperbolic embeddings, against which the training-free exact tree is measured; Explainer 6 put its trainer on the CPU track. Phase 4 built it and decided the two claims about it. This page’s focus node is the static embeddings.

The code is in reference/: the trainer lexicon/embed.py (549 lines), its stream stream.py (201, a numba copy of the shared Rng) and pystream.py (91, the same calls on hpstep.prng.Rng), the snapping and exact evaluation learned.py (633), the controls, intervals and decisions l6.py (746), and the runner run_claims_phase4.py (2,230). The browser core’s exact ranking of snapped tables is the Phase 4 section of lexicon_core.js (lines 1,573–1,837 of its 3,205: SnapArm, evaluateSnapped and the L6 trace), and run_l6_node.cjs (416) re-ranked the measured tables in Node. Every number on this page comes from results/claims-phase4.json (summarised in claims-phase4.md), tuning.json, l6-node.json (.md), fractional-ties.json (.md), the dry run in results/dry/, PREREGISTRATION.md (L4, L6, Amendments 10, 11 and 12), the commit messages and the source files; the lab’s values come from your own browser. Where this page and those files differ, the files win.

How to read this page

supported means what the registered decision rule says, and nothing more. L6 is statistical: per seed, the paired per-node differences in average precision between two tables, their mean and a 95% bootstrap interval, in each of five seeds, read by the rule of each part. L4(c) is exact: a largest difference of MAPs, computed as rationals, against 1/1000. A prediction was written with each claim; it is not part of the rule, and one of this phase’s was wrong.

Decimals are rounded from the exact values in the record. Times belong to this container and decide nothing; they are marked “~” where they stand alone.

Closure set S
mammal.n.01 (01861778) and every noun with it among its hypernym and instance-hypernym ancestors: 1,182 synsets, in offset order, with 6,542 (node, ancestor) pairs inside S. SHA-256 of its offsets c4748ce2….
Closure MAP
Every node is a query against the other 1,181. Its neighbours are its closure ancestors and descendants inside S (13,084 query–neighbour pairs). The MAP is the mean of the 1,182 average precisions.
Graph-edge MAP
The neighbours are the spanning parent (the first hypernym or instance-hypernym pointer) and the spanning children, inside S: 2,350 pairs over 1,176 queries. Six nodes have their spanning parent outside S and no children in S; they have no AP and stay candidates.
Pessimistic ties
A neighbour tied exactly with a non-neighbour ranks after it (the Common rules). Fractional ties average the AP over every order inside each tie class; they are reported, never used to decide.
Learned table
1,182 rows of float64 after epoch 999 of a run: a Lorentz row (x0, x1, …, xd) on the hyperboloid, a Euclidean row (x1, …, xd).
Snapped table
The table on the 2−32 grid, as integers n: for Lorentz, of the Poincaré point p = x/(1 + x0), n = ⌊232p + 1/2⌋ by fence.dyadic, stepped in at the rim; for Euclidean, n = ⌊232x + 1/2⌋ of the float read exactly. Its text, a line offset n_1 … n_d per node, is committed and named by its SHA-256.
κ key
For a query u and candidate v of a snapped Lorentz table, κu(v) = |nu − nv|²/(432 − |nv|²): an exact rational that orders and ties the candidates exactly as the Lorentz product of the lifted points does (A10.6). A Euclidean table’s key is |nu − nv|².
Cell, seed
A cell is a geometry and a dimension: Lorentz or Euclidean at d = 2, 3, 5, 10 or 50, ten in all. A run is a cell, a learning rate and a seed; its draws come from one Rng(seed).
Interval
Per seed, the 95% percentile bootstrap of the mean per-node paired AP difference, 10,000 resamples with numpy’s default_rng(7), started afresh for each interval (A10.7).
Seed present
A seed counts for a part when the tables it compares exist (no run diverged) and control 2 is established for their cells at that seed, that is, both runs of its pair exist (A10.7; Amendment 11 item 4). A failing control 2 does not drop a seed; it voids all of L6. A part is supported only with all five seeds present.
Exact arm
L3’s H³ words of S, placed exactly by the reference’s h3tree.py (hpstep.decode3d’s octahedral tree; decoder3d.js’s pointExact is its JavaScript twin, against which the Node re-evaluation checked the arm’s lines) and ranked by exact Lorentz products: the training-free tree, with no seed.
Dry run
The whole pipeline on the closure set of solid.n.01 (1,232 nouns, 5,696 closure edges), every seed +3000 (A10.13). It decides nothing.
Decisive run
The one run that decides: run_claims_phase4.py --measure --workers 4, on the code committed at 515a4a1.

With these, all eleven registered claims are supported: L1–L11, L3 and L4 in full (Phase 1 in Explainer 9, Phase 2 in Explainer 10, Phase 3 in Explainer 11). Six labels to note

“Amendments 1–11”. The decisive record and the Node re-evaluation’s both say they ran “with PREREGISTRATION.md with Amendments 1-11”. The A10.17 supplement ran before tuning. A10.17 asked for the fractional-tie values of Phases 1 and 2, on those runs’ own code and data; A10.14 says nothing may be ranked on the mammal sets before registered tuning. The supplement ran during the build (at 43265cf, again at 272a50d after a fix to learned.py, every value unchanged), so it ranked Phase 1’s arms on the mammal spanning subtree first. Amendment 11 item 3 discloses the conflict.

Tuning previews the outcome. As A10.14 says, tuning on the mammals shows Lorentz against Euclidean closure MAP before any measured run: at its seeds 9000–9023 the chosen Lorentz rates scored 0.885–0.968 and the Euclidean ones 0.167–0.99997. That is why every line of code and the whole dry run came before it.

The dry run saw (c) come out supported. On solid.n.01, about half an hour before registered tuning began (the dry measure ended at about 17:42, tuning started at 18:15:39) and about two hours before the decisive run, the dry run’s exact tree scored graph-edge MAP 0.619361 against learned Lorentz d = 3 at 0.497–0.520, and its verdict on (c) was supported. Dry data decide nothing; (c)’s prediction is registered text, which no amendment edits, and it was left as it stood.

Amendment 11’s closest choice. Amendment 11 item 1 named Lorentz d = 2 the closest tuning choice (rates 1 and 3: 0.884987 and 0.884130, 0.000857 apart). By that same measure Euclidean d = 50 is closer (rates 3 and 1: 0.999973 and 0.999840, 0.000133). Amendment 12 corrects the sentence; it decides nothing, since A10.8 picks each cell’s rate by the larger exact mean alone.

The geoopt cross-check was not run. D9 names geoopt as a cross-check of the Lorentz step. torch could not be installed through the proxy (download.pytorch.org refused; PyPI’s Linux wheel pulls more than 1 GB of CUDA wheels), as A10.11 foresaw; the tests carry a 60-digit decimal recomputation of one step and finite-difference gradients in its place. It would have been a tolerance, never a decision.

03

The protocol

L6 registered the training as Nickel and Kiela’s. Amendment 6 found that its constants mix Nickel and Kiela’s Poincaré paper (P17) with their released script and add one of this project’s own (1,000 epochs), and A10.15 says where every constant comes from, P18 included. Each run is a function of the geometry, d, the rate, the seed and the instance alone.

The training rule

ElementThe rule (A10.2–5)Where it comes from (A10.15)
PositivesThe 6,542 pairs (u, v) with v a closure ancestor of u, in one direction: u is the anchorThe script’s (child, ancestor) orientation

GradientsTo the anchor, the positive and every negative, repeats summed; every node met in a batch takes one step, from the table as it stood at the batch’s startThe script’s rsgd.py lines 53–57

P17 is Nickel and Kiela’s Poincaré paper (arXiv:1705.08039v2), P18 their Lorentz paper (arXiv:1806.03417v2), and the script facebookresearch/poincare-embeddings at dff101b. Also not followed from the script: negatives drawn without replacement and padded, the always-on max-norm of 106, initial ranges of ±10−5 (Lorentz) and ±10−4 (Euclidean), and five Hogwild threads. The only mammal number found in the literature, P17’s Figure 2 (Poincaré, d = 5: MAP 0.927), is not comparable: its instance is unknown, its ranking directed and its ties not pessimistic. No claim compares against it.

The evaluation

  • Snap, then rank exactly. Each table is snapped to 2−32 (the glossary’s snapped table) and every node ranked against the other 1,181 by the κ key (Lorentz) or the exact squared distance (Euclidean), with rank._ap_and_ranks unchanged: pessimistic APs, MAP and pooled mean rank as exact rationals.
  • Per seed, per part. The per-node AP differences of the two tables, in offset order (1,182 for closure, 1,176 for graph-edge); their mean and 95% interval. The exact arm has no seed, so its APs enter every seed’s comparison.
  • The rules, as registered. (a) at each of d = 2, 3: supported if the interval lies above 0 in all five seeds; refuted if the mean is ≤ 0 in three or more. (b) at d = 50: supported if every interval lies within [−0.02, +0.02] or entirely below 0; refuted if above +0.02 in three or more. (c) supported if every lower end is ≥ −0.01; refuted if the mean is below −0.05 in three or more. (d) supported if every interval lies above 0; refuted if the mean is ≤ 0 in three or more. Otherwise inconclusive; a failed control voids all four. No multiplicity correction is registered, and none is applied.
  • Tuning (A10.8): in each of the ten cells, rate k of {0.03, 0.1, 0.3, 1, 3, 10} trained on seeds 9000 + 4k to 9003 + 4k; the rate with the largest exact mean closure MAP over its four runs is frozen, the smaller on a tie; a rate with a diverged run is not eligible. Measured runs use seeds 0–4.

How it is computed. The tables are the committed snapped texts results/l6-tables/learned-lorentz-d2-s0.snap.txt.gz and learned-euclid-d2-s0.snap.txt.gz, carried to the page by data/l6_view_v1.js with each synset’s first lemma and parents. A Lorentz table is SnapArm(N, 'lorentz'): candidates ordered by κu(v), compared by BigInt cross-multiplication; a Euclidean one, SnapArm(N, 'euclid'), by |nu − nv|². Each query goes through rankQuery and the MAP through mapExact, the functions evaluateSnapped and the Node re-evaluation use. Closure neighbours are rebuilt from the closure parents, graph-edge neighbours from the spanning parents. A rank is 1 plus the non-neighbours nearer than that neighbour, ties pessimistic (these two tables have none). Drawing alone uses floats: the disk’s points are n/232, moved, when asked, by the hyperbolic isometry that brings the mammal picked to the centre, then spread as Explainer 3’s lab does (at ×s each distance from the centre is drawn divided by s, directions kept; at ×1 the picture is the true disk, where almost every point lies on the rim). Edges are drawn straight.

How the lab gets its tables, and what it checks

A page here uses no fetch, so it works from file:// as well as over HTTP: reference/golden/make_l6_view_v1.py writes data/l6_view_v1.js from the two committed snapped texts, claims-phase4.json and the WordNet archive, after checking that S, its closure and graph-edge neighbour sets and both tables are the decisive run’s; its --check rebuilds the file and compares the bytes. On load the page checks the file’s JSON text against its SHA-256; rebuilds S’s offsets and checks their SHA-256 (c4748ce2…), the 6,542 closure edges and the neighbour counts against the record; rebuilds each snapped text from the integers and checks its SHA-256 against the run’s snap_sha; and checks each recorded MAP text against its LBENCH/1 hash. The MAPs it computes are compared with those texts. The first lemmas and parents are WordNet 3.0’s, under the WordNet licence (its notice).

Try it
  • mammal sits at the centre of the disk, 0.21 from the origin in hyperbolic distance. Every other node is its closure neighbour, so its closure AP is 1 in both tables, whatever the geometry.
  • Samoyed, a leaf six steps down: closure AP 0.948 in the Lorentz table, 0.112 in the Euclidean one. In the disk its nearest candidates are spitz, its parent, then carnivore, mammal and placental, ancestors at every level; in the plane they are other breeds of dog, none of them its closure neighbour but spitz, second.
  • dog, with 189 descendants: 0.621 in the Lorentz table and 0.987 in the Euclidean one. The plane serves hubs and fails leaves; 889 of the 1,182 nodes are leaves. Recompute both MAPs and the lab counts the queries each table ranks better.
  • lion: its spanning parent, big cat, ranks 7th in the disk, behind three of its ancestors (mammal, placental, carnivore), one of its descendants (lion cub, whose spanning parent lies outside S) and two nodes outside its closure. Training made every ancestor a positive with the same weight, so the parent has no special place: this is L6(c) on one query (chapter 6).
  • tusker, a child of mammal with no children of its own: 0.5 in the disk (mammal ranks 2nd), 0.0038 in the plane (261st).
  • Centre the disk on Samoyed. Every other mammal then crowds into one narrow direction: seen from a leaf near the rim, the rest of the tree lies far off, toward the origin. The hyperbolic plane gives each branch room by putting it far from every other.
  • Spread ×1 shows the true disk: the farthest point of this table lies 16.7 from the origin (the record’s max_radius), within 2×10−7 of the rim in the Poincaré coordinates that were snapped.
What this lab is, and is not

It runs the functions the Node re-evaluation ran, on two of the tables it checked, and recomputes their MAPs; your browser’s numbers decide nothing. L6 was decided by the Python reference on all 50 measured tables, five seeds a cell; these are two tables of one seed, at the dimension where the geometries differ most. Its drawings are pictures in floats; only the ranking is exact. A Euclidean table in two dimensions is a plane with no centre; it is drawn around the origin, at one scale.

The wrong prediction, and why

L6(c): predicted refuted, measured supported

The prediction was fixed in Phase 0 by a rule set with the design and applied to spike S3 on the disjoint verb forest: “supported if S3’s exact-tree graph-edge MAP on verbs is ≥ 0.90, otherwise refuted”. S3 measured 0.6309, so the registered prediction read refuted (Explainer 8). The rule judged the exact tree against a fixed bar, and so took for granted that learned Lorentz embeddings keep graph edges well, near that bar, and that a tree held back by its virtual letters (siblings that share a virtual prefix sit as close as the parent, or closer) would fall below them.

They do not keep graph edges. The training signal is the closure: each of a node’s ancestors is a positive with the same weight, so mammal.n.01 and placental.n.01 are pulled toward a node as hard as its direct parent is, and its siblings are negatives no more than any other non-neighbour. Trained on the closure, the learned tables rank ancestors and descendants alike; the direct parent and children are a few among them. Their graph-edge MAP is 0.458–0.477 at d = 3 and 0.432–0.462 at every other d, and their pooled graph-edge mean rank 15.06–19.04 against the exact tree’s 2.909. The exact tree, low for the reason S3 found, is still well above: 0.690653.

The decision follows the rule, not the prediction: (c) is supported. The prediction stays on record as wrong, beside L2’s and L11’s. The lab shows it on one query: lion’s parent ranks 7th in the disk, behind three of its ancestors and three other nodes.

(d)’s prediction, “supported: learning pulls ancestors toward the origin, which closure ranking rewards”, held. The record decides the outcome and measures no mechanism. The lab shows where one table puts its nodes: pick a mammal and it gives the distance from the origin. In the lab’s Lorentz table (d = 2, seed 0), mammal.n.01 and placental.n.01, the two most general, sit 0.21 and 0.30 from it, and every other node at 2.7 or more; in the d = 3 table of seed 0, which (d) compares, 0.13, 0.17 and 2.85 or more.

What this does not show

Five supported parts about one hierarchy, one protocol and one machine. They say a good deal about how geometry and training shape a learned table, and nothing about language.

  • WordNet mammals only. One instance: 1,182 synsets, nine levels deep, nearly a tree (one node has two parents inside S). Other hierarchies, deeper or wider, all nouns, verbs, are new tests.
  • Reconstruction, not link prediction. Every closure edge that is scored was trained on. (a), (b) and (d) measure how well each geometry fits the relation it was given, not whether it predicts an edge it never saw. No edge was held out.
  • No lexical meaning. The tables place synset ids. Lemmas, glosses and the way words are used play no part, and no word’s meaning is tested.
  • The Stage-2 lexical claims were not registered. The roadmap listed them for Phase 4 by amendment: HyperLex, a hypernymy suite, SimLex-999 and the other similarity and relatedness controls (Explainer 7). No such amendment was written, and no code for them exists. They remain to be registered by amendment before any code for them.
  • One protocol, one tuning. The protocol is this project’s own, after Nickel and Kiela’s papers and script (A10.15); another protocol may order the geometries differently, and these numbers are not comparable with theirs. Each cell’s rate was tuned on the same instance at other seeds, on closure MAP alone, from a six-point grid. Under A10.8’s reading of “in blocks of four”, each rate ran on its own block of four seeds (240 runs, not every rate on all 24 seeds, 1,440 runs). So rates were compared across different seeds, and the two closest choices (Euclidean d = 50, 0.000133; Lorentz d = 2, 0.000857) could fall the other way on other seeds.
  • (c) is one comparison. It sets the exact tree against learned Lorentz at d = 3, as registered. Learned Euclidean at d = 50 has a graph-edge MAP of 0.850–0.854, above the exact tree’s 0.691 (reported). A learned table trained on graph edges rather than the closure was not part of L6.
  • One machine. Python 3.13.18, numpy 2.5.3, numba 0.68.1, glibc 2.39, 4 CPUs; bit identity is promised on this build machine only, and the record names its environment. The geoopt cross-check was not run.
  • Snapping at 2−32 only. L4(c) says that this grid keeps the ranking of these tables; it says nothing about whether a table is good, and a coarse grid does not keep it.
  • The lab draws two tables of one seed and recomputes their MAPs with the functions the Node re-evaluation used. It demonstrates the code and decides nothing.
