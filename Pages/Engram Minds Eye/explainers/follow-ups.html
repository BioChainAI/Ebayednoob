03

E10, a sharper guard

E8 found the guard naming a corrupted stored distance right 96 times in 100 at +1.5, short of the 99 its rule asked for. Under E8’s rule more instances could not settle that, so E10 asked a sharper question.

The proposition, proved in the registration: raise one distance of a matrix that has no quadruple above ln 2. Every quadruple that then exceeds ln 2 contains the corrupted pair, inside its largest matching sum. So the guard, which counts all six pairs of each such quadruple, always gives the corrupted pair the top count. It can miss only by a tie, or when no quadruple exceeds ln 2. The matching guard counts only the two pairs of the largest matching, and so names the pair alone whenever two or more quadruples exceed ln 2.

The prediction that missed

The registration predicted E10b supported. It is not: in E8’s setting at +1.5 the matching guard names the pair 96.5 times in 100, with an interval reaching 0.94, below the 0.95 the rule asks of its lower end and short of 0.99.

04

What E10 means

Look at the third and fifth columns: in every cell, the matching guard names the pair exactly as often as two or more quadruples exceed ln 2. The limit is not in how blame is shared out. It is in how many witnesses the corruption leaves.

  • Why E8 fell short. At +1.5 in E8’s setting the guard missed 8 of 200 instances. One was a tie with 13 quadruples above ln 2, all sharing a third point; the matching guard names that one. In the other seven, raising the distance pushed at most one quadruple over ln 2. With no witness, nothing can be named. With one, that quadruple accuses two pairs equally, and no counting can choose between them.
  • Why the bound leaves so little room. A clean matrix of this tree already comes within 0.001 of ln 2 (its worst defect is 0.6923). Some distances can grow by 1.5 and still leave every quadruple under the bound, or just one over it.
  • What it changes. E8 stays inconclusive: E10 explains it but does not redecide it. Under E10’s rule the watch layer keeps the guard as it is. The 3D page now shows, for a corrupted distance, how many quadruples exceed ln 2 and what both guards blame. When there is a single witness it says the blame is split between two pairs, instead of naming one.
  • What would settle it. A claim about naming when two or more quadruples exceed ln 2 would be decided by the proposition alone. A claim that a guard names the pair from fewer witnesses would need more than four-point counts, such as the stored distances’ own history. Neither is registered.
