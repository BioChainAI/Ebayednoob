CHAPTER 1

What the simulation shows

The Galactic Lensing Hierarchy treats the centre of the Milky Way as three nested systems, each organized like one copy of the geometric engine, and keeps exact books on what the three of them explain and what they leave over.

Every measurement of a pulsar near the Galactic Centre (where it appears on the sky, how its spin period drifts, how fast it moves) is shaped by mass at every scale between the black hole and the edge of the Galaxy. The simulation splits that mass into three ranges of radius:

System I

The Sgr A* domain, from the Schwarzschild radius to 2 pc: the black hole itself.

System II

The nuclear region, 2 to 200 pc: the nuclear star cluster, gas and the plasma screen.

System III

The Galaxy, 200 pc to 100 kpc: bar, disks, halo, and the Sun's own motion.

Whatever the three models fail to explain is the emergent fourth, written \(Y_R\). It has no mass model of its own. It is defined as data minus the three models, so the four always add up to the measurement exactly. This is the additive form of the monograph's implied fourth (Lemma 7 Part 3(d)).

Each measurement comes in three classes (astrometry, timing and velocity), and each class splits into the same four parts: I, II, III and R. Three classes of four make twelve streams. That is the triple quad: the monograph's twelve-engine hierarchy of three groups of four (§7.4, Proposition 7.11), applied to the galaxy.

In plain words

One engine is a set of nested rings with a clock and a 64-bit message format. Three engines side by side can be checked against each other, because any three of a group of four fix the fourth. The simulation uses that one idea twice: to split each measurement into "explained by I, II, III" plus "unexplained", and to check that the coordinate frames the data pass through fit together.

The page has two layers, as the design specification requires:

  • Layer A, physics. Weak-field general relativity in ordinary floating point: lensing, Shapiro delay and accelerations. These are approximate, as all physics is.
  • Layer B, bookkeeping. Exact rational arithmetic in JavaScript BigInt: the residual parity, the quaternion frame closure, the packet layout and the Merkle chain. Layer B adds no error of its own. It cannot make a measurement more certain, only make sure nothing is lost or invented on the way.
CHAPTER 2

The route in five steps

The whole guide follows one path. Step through it here first; each stage has its own chapter later.

From one engine to the triple quad
  1. One engine. Five tiers in disjoint shells, a 30-node wheel, six channels, a 12/10/8 gear clock, an 8-step cycle, and a 64-bit packet (Lemmas 4, 8, 10, 11 and 12, §6.1). Chapters 4–7.
  2. A fourth from three. Three holonomies fix a fourth, \(H_4 = (H_1H_2H_3)^{-1}\). It is a parity element that rebuilds any one lost member (Lemma 7 Part 3(d)). It tests something only if measured independently (Lemma 12). Chapter 8.
  3. Many engines. Engines tile space without touching, and exchange data only at the two handshake windows of the cycle (Theorems 7.1–7.10). Chapter 9.
  4. Twelve engines, three groups of four. Nine data engines and three implied fourths: the triple quad. One loss per group is recoverable (§7.4, Propositions 7.11–7.14). Chapter 10.
  5. The galaxy. Systems I–III are three engines, the residual is the fourth, and three observable classes make three groups of four. Frame closure and the four-structure frame change are the same algebra again (§7.6, Theorem 7.18). Chapters 12–15.

Five tiers and their shells

The engine is five nested tori, one per tier, centred on the origin. All lengths are in cubits \(\mathcal{C}_0 = \lambda/16\), about 53.7 cm at 40 Hz in room air (§6.2).

Lemma 4 fixes the tier laws for \(n = 0, \dots, 4\):

$$R_n = 5n^2 + 15n + 15, \qquad \rho_n = 2n + 4, \qquad W_n = 16 - 2n,$$

where \(R_n\) is the major radius, \(\rho_n\) the minor (tube) radius and \(W_n\) the number of helical windings. Two conservation laws follow at once, and the page checks both on every load PROVEN:

$$\rho_n + W_n = 20 \text{ for every tier}, \qquad \sum_{n=0}^{4} W_n = W_\Sigma = 60.$$

Tier frames: Lemma 4 versus engine v10

Lemma 4 also fixes how each tier is oriented: \(\mathrm{Rot}(T_n) = \mathrm{Rot}_x(\pi/2)\) for even \(n\) and \(\mathrm{Rot}_y(\pi/2)\) for odd \(n\). The step from one tier to the next is then always the same integer matrix,

$$\mathbf U_{\text{even}} = \mathrm{Rot}_y(\tfrac{\pi}{2})\,\mathrm{Rot}_x(-\tfrac{\pi}{2}) = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & -1 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \\ -1 & 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix},$$

a 120° rotation about the body diagonal \((-1,1,1)/\sqrt3\), with \(\mathbf U^3 = I\). Its spinor lift is the Hurwitz unit \(h = (1 - i + j + k)/2\), with \(h^3 = -1\) and \(h^6 = +1\) PROVEN.

Engine v10 uses a different, simpler set of frames: tiers 0, 2 and 4 unrotated and tiers 1 and 3 turned by \(\mathrm{Rot}_x(\pi/2)\), plus the tension, torsion and clutch-slip offsets of its controls. The simulation draws the pure Lemma 4 frames by default: exactly \(\mathrm{Rot}_x(\pi/2)\) and \(\mathrm{Rot}_y(\pi/2)\), with none of the engine's tension, torsion or clutch-slip offsets. In this mode the clutch changes only how fast the helices spin, not how the tiers are tilted. Panel D checks the drawn frames live: every step \(q_{n+1}q_n^{-1}\) between neighbouring tiers must be the 120° turn about \(\pm(-1,1,1)/\sqrt3\). The Lemma 4 frames button switches to the v10 frames, offsets included, for comparison. In the galaxy the tier rotations have no physical meaning (the specification's verdict is "no map"); they only make the nesting visible.

Why the frequencies are 96, 84, 72, 60, 48 Hz

Engine v10 drives tier \(n\) at \(f_n = 6W_n\) Hz. This is the engine's own choice DEFINITION, not a lemma. It does interact with Lemma 11's clock windows, though: on the 60-pulse register, a carrier at \(f\) Hz returns to a whole period every \(60/\gcd(60, f)\) pulses, so all five tiers line up every 5 pulses (1/12 s). The simulation checks this exactly (Chapter 5).

What the 60-pulse register cannot do

Proposition 11.4 gives the exact rule: integer carriers \(f_1, \dots, f_r\) share a window of \(k\) pulses if and only if \(\mathrm{lcm}_i\big(60/\gcd(60, f_i)\big)\) divides \(k\). The caesium frequency 9 192 631 770 Hz is \(\equiv 30 \pmod{60}\), so it closes on every second pulse. Pulsar spin frequencies are not integers, so the rule says nothing about them. The register schedules packets; pulsar data are folded on each pulsar's own ephemeris against TT(BIPM) (spec §4.7).

The 8-step cycle

Every engine runs an eight-step cycle \(\theta_k = k\pi/4\). Two of its steps are special: they are the only moments at which two engines can exchange data.

Lemma 8 builds the cycle step by step: a circle phase (Step 1), the conformal lift with nine rays and a 4-simplex (Step 2), sixteen half-cubes (Step 3), dilation to the voxel cube (Step 4), a sense-reversing fold into Cube 3 (Step 5), the \(D_3\) trefoil and its projection into hyperbolic space (Step 6), and the apex peaks and holonomy (Steps 7–8).

Its spinor bookkeeping is exact. One step is a 90° rotation with spinor \(1 + i\), and Lemma 13 Part 4 shows that in integer arithmetic

$$(1+i)^2 = 2i, \qquad (1+i)^4 = -4, \qquad (1+i)^8 = 16.$$

Dividing each power by the gcd of its parts leaves the eight unit states \(1+i,\ i,\ -1+i,\ -1,\ -1-i,\ -i,\ 1-i,\ 1\). The transverse amplitude \(|\sin\theta_k|\) is 1 only at \(k = 2\) and \(k = 6\), where the reduced spinors are \(i\) and \(-i\): the same half-turn about the \(x\)-axis with opposite spin sign PROVEN (Theorem 7.5). These are the two handshake windows.

CHAPTER 8 · LEMMA 7, LEMMA 12, THEOREM 7.2

The fourth from three

The single idea behind the whole hierarchy: in a group of four that must close, any three determine the fourth. That makes the fourth a spare, able to rebuild one loss. It also means a fourth computed from the other three can never catch an error.

Already inside one engine: V4 and V4*

Engine v10 triangulates each node against an upper and a lower tier point (V1, V2, V3) and infers a fourth vertex V4. Its exact parallelogram closure is \(V_4^* = V_2 + V_3 - V_1\), so that \(V_1 + V_4^* = V_2 + V_3\): one point of four, fixed by the other three. The engine's displayed V4 scales that closure by its TQF factor qScale, which is an engine heuristic HEURISTIC. The Numogram page draws both side by side.

The group form: Lemma 7 Part 3

For any three holonomy quaternions \(H_1, H_2, H_3 \in S^3\), there is exactly one \(H_4\) that closes the loop PROVEN:

$$H_1 H_2 H_3 H_4 = 1 \iff H_4 = (H_1H_2H_3)^{-1}.$$

Part 3(d) reads this as a parity element. Since the closure survives cyclic shifts, each of the four is fixed by the other three, so one lost element of a group of four is recoverable exactly; two are not. \(H_4\) carries no information beyond its three parents. Offloading it returns only their product, never their individual histories.

The lattice form: Theorem 7.2

Take the lattice \(L = 2\mathbb Z^3\) and the four offsets \(o_0 = (0,0,0)\), \(o_1 = (1,1,0)\), \(o_2 = (1,0,1)\), \(o_3 = (0,1,1)\). The four translates \(L + o_i\) are disjoint and together make the face-centred cubic lattice \(D_3\). Since \(o_1 + o_2 + o_3 \equiv 0 \pmod 2\), any three translates determine the fourth: the "3:1 interleave" PROVEN. The monograph notes that the implied fourth has three exact twins: this lattice, the group inverse \(H_4\), and the quaternion relation \(ijk = -1\).

The additive form: what the galaxy uses

Measured quantities add. With a measured total \(Y\) and three model contributions, the fourth is the residual \(Y_R = Y - Y_I - Y_{II} - Y_{III}\), and the group closes as \(Y = Y_I + Y_{II} + Y_{III} + Y_R\). That is Proposition G1 of the specification, the additive form of Lemma 7 Part 3(d) (Chapter 13).

CHAPTER 10 · MONOGRAPH §7.4, PROPOSITIONS 7.11–7.14

Twelve engines: the triple quad

Apply "the fourth from three" three times, and you have twelve engines in three groups of four. This is the structure the galaxy reuses.

Definition (§7.4). A six-tier engine is the core plus the five tiers of Lemma 4. Its memory state is its holonomy \(H \in S^3\) together with its 64-bit packet. Three data engines \(A_1, A_2, A_3\) determine an implied fourth \(F_A = (A_1A_2A_3)^{-1}\). Doing this three times (groups A, B and C) gives nine data engines and three implied fourths: twelve engines, three groups of four DEFINITION. An optional thirteenth engine \(F_\star = (F_AF_BF_C)^{-1}\) is the fourth of the fourths.

Parity (Proposition 7.11)

Each group of four tolerates the loss of any one engine, so the hierarchy survives up to three losses, one per group. Two losses in the same group are unrecoverable. Only 9 of the 12 engines carry data (75%). PROVEN

The thirteenth engine

Adds exactly one recovery case: losing a data engine together with its own fourth (say \(A_1\) and \(F_A\)), since \(F_\star\), \(F_B\), \(F_C\) rebuild \(F_A\), which then rebuilds \(A_1\). PROVEN

Exact offload (Proposition 7.12)

Onload carries data out through the tier word \(W = U_3U_2U_1U_0\). Its inverse is again a word in \(U\) and \(U^{-1}\), so the inverse tier system is exact. The radial scale \(R_4/R_0 = 155/15 = 31/3\) is exact both ways. PROVEN

The third phase (Proposition 7.14)

Three channels \((a, b, c)\) in three 120° phases are one invariant line \(s = a+b+c\) plus one rotating plane \(z = a + \omega b + \omega^2 c\). Onload and offload use two phases; the third can carry the parity traffic, a 4/3 data-rate gain. PROVEN

CHAPTER 11 · MONOGRAPH §7.6–§7.7, THEOREMS 7.18–7.20

The four-structure system

The monograph has a second "three plus an emergent fourth": an input sandwiched between two balancers, and the structure that closes the sandwich. The galaxy uses it as a frame-change check.

Definition (§7.6). Let \(A\) be a twelve-engine structure with holonomies \(A_1, \dots, A_{12}\), and \(\bar A\) its dynamical inverse, \(\bar A_j = A_j^{-1}\). A third structure \(I\) carries the input. The balancers \(A\) and \(\bar A\) sit on either side of the input, and the emergent structure \(E\) is fixed by the slot-wise closure

$$A_j\, I_j\, A_j^{-1}\, E_j = 1, \qquad j = 1, \dots, 12.$$

Theorem 7.18. \(E_j = A\,I_j^{-1}A^{-1}\), so \(\mathrm{Re}(E_j) = \mathrm{Re}(I_j)\) PROVEN. Conjugation keeps the real part of a unit quaternion, which fixes the rotation angle; only the axes turn. Had the balancers been placed next to each other (\(I\,A\,A^{-1}\,E = 1\)), they would cancel and give \(E = I^{-1}\).

Theorem 7.19. A product in which every factor also appears inverted equals 1 for all values only if it reduces to the empty word by cancelling adjacent inverse pairs: forward and inverse streams cancel only when nested. Proposition 7.20. If \(I_1I_2I_3I_4 = 1\) in a group of four, then \(E_4E_3E_2E_1 = 1\): the emergent structure closes its own groups, read in reverse PROVEN.

Conjugation keeps the angle

The 64 streams and the four projection checks (§7.7)

The emergent fourth

This chapter is where the galaxy's triple quad appears: three classes of measurement, each a group of four.

Proposition G1 (additive residual parity) PROVEN. Let \(Y\) be a weak-field observable (a Shapiro delay change, a deflection, an astrometric shift, or the acceleration term in a pulsar's period derivative), and \(Y_s\) its value computed from system \(s\)'s mass model. Define \(Y_R := Y - Y_I - Y_{II} - Y_{III}\). Then:

  1. \(Y = Y_I + Y_{II} + Y_{III} + Y_R\) exactly, in rational arithmetic.
  2. Any one of the four is recovered exactly from \(Y\) and the other three.
  3. Two unknowns with one total are not recoverable: a whole line of pairs fits (script check H1 finds 50 distinct consistent pairs).

Superposition is what makes the parity physical: at every known pulsar the Sgr A* field has \(GM/c^2r \le 2.1 \times 10^{-6}\), so second-order terms are a fraction of about \(10^{-6}\) of the first-order ones COMPUTED. Only inside a few \(r_s\) does superposition fail (Problem G4).

CHAPTER 15 · SPEC §4.7, §5.3, LEMMA 15

Packets, phases and the Merkle chain

Each update, the twelve cells of the triple quad leave as twelve 64-bit tiles, and the twelve are chained into one 256-bit root.

  1. Pack. For class \(c\) and structure \(s\) (I, II, III or R), stream slot \(4c + s\) gets one Form 3 tile with the fields of Chapter 7. The cycle step in bits 63–61 says whether this update is onload, offload or third phase.
  2. Leaf. Leaf \(G_d\) (for \(d = 1, \dots, 12\)) is the 8 bytes of tile \(d - 1\) followed by its exact rational written as text, so the leaf covers the exact value even when binary32 rounds it.
  3. Chain (Lemma 15 Part 3). \(\tau_{12} = H(G_{12})\) and \(\tau_d = H(G_d \,\|\, \tau_{d+1})\) for \(d = 11, \dots, 1\), with \(H\) = SHA-256. The root \(\tau_1\) changes if anything in any leaf changes, unless SHA-256 has a collision PROVEN.
  4. Log. Once per epoch the telemetry log records a [B5] line with the step, the phase, how many values binary32 holds exactly, and the root.

Lemma 15 Part 3's proof also says where a change sits. If two states differ but their roots agree, the deepest layer \(d^\ast\) at which their chain values first agree exhibits an explicit SHA-256 collision. In the other direction, a change in leaf \(d\) changes every \(\tau\) from \(\tau_d\) up to the root. Panel D's Tamper button changes one leaf's exact value by \(2^{-24}\) and reports what each layer sees. Usually the tile word does not change (binary32 cannot see so small a change), the South Halo never does, and the root always does.

Why the 256-bit root and not the tile

A 64-bit label for a 12-layer state of \(2^{72}\) possibilities has at least \(2^8 = 256\) preimages (Lemma 15 Part 1), so no 64-bit word can certify the state. Linear checksums such as CRC-32 give no integrity at all, since \(\mathrm{crc}(x \oplus y \oplus z) = \mathrm{crc}(x) \oplus \mathrm{crc}(y) \oplus \mathrm{crc}(z)\). Integrity needs a collision-resistant hash, chained.

How Form 1 encodes a quaternion (monograph Table 5)

  1. Clear denominators. Multiply by the common denominator \(L\). The rotation \(\rho_Q(x) = QxQ^{\ast}/N(Q)\) does not change when \(Q\) is scaled (Lemma 13 Part 3), so \(LQ\) is the same rotation in integers.
  2. Double. Take \(2Q\), so Hurwitz half-integer quaternions such as \(h = (1 - i + j + k)/2\) also become integers.
  3. Reduce. Divide by the gcd of the four coordinates.
  4. Normalize the sign. Make the first non-zero coordinate positive. \(Q\) and \(-Q\) give the same rotation; the spin sign is carried by the cycle step in bits 63–61, not by the payload.
  5. Fit or flag. If \(2w \le 127\) and \(|x|, |y|, |z| \le 127\), the bytes hold the quaternion exactly and the loss flag stays clear. Otherwise the coordinates are rescaled so the largest is 127, rounded to the nearest representable direction, and the loss flag is set.

Why the measured legs never fit

Form 1 is built for the engine's own Hurwitz arithmetic: units such as \(h\), small integer quaternions, and the cycle spinors \((1+i)^k\), which Table 5 notes never overflow. A measured frame leg is a different animal. It starts as a floating-point rotation, is rationalized at \(2^{-20}\) to \(2^{-40}\) (plus \(2^{-48}\) for the noise in \(H_4\)), and passes through the Cayley map, so its exact coordinates run to 40–180 bits. Lemma 13 Part 1 says denominators of rational unit quaternions grow without bound, so no fixed-width field can hold every such rotation. A 7-bit grid resolves directions only to roughly 1/127 per coordinate, a few tenths of a degree. The loss flag is how Form 1 says so honestly.

The cross-engine Triple-Quad Formula

For each class, the three nodes \(P_{\text{I}}, P_{\text{II}}, P_{\text{III}}\) are rationalized at \(2^{-24}\) in each engine's own cubit frame, then turned exactly. $$A_R = (Q_1 + Q_2 + Q_3)^2 - 2(Q_1^2 + Q_2^2 + Q_3^2) = 4\big(|u|^2|v|^2 - \langle u, v\rangle^2\big), \quad u = P_{\text{II}} - P_{\text{I}},\ v = P_{\text{III}} - P_{\text{I}}.$$

Both are computed in BigInt, and the page checks they agree. Note that \(|u|^2 = Q_1\) and \(|v|^2 = Q_3\): the Gram form pairs the two edges that meet at \(P_{\text{I}}\).

Lock. Weitzenböck's inequality (Lemma 4 Part 2) says \(\chi = \sqrt{A_R}/(Q_1+Q_2+Q_3) \le 1/\sqrt3\), with equality exactly for an equilateral triangle. When the three phase offsets agree, the three nodes are \(P, \sigma P, \sigma^2 P\) for one rational point \(P\). Since \(\sigma\) is orthogonal, \(Q_1 = Q_2 = Q_3\) exactly and \(3A_R = S^2\): the triangle is equilateral, with no rounding involved. That equality is the page's lock. Any difference between the systems' residuals deforms the triangle for that class only. A lensing event on the magnetar, for example, deforms astrometry and timing but leaves velocity locked.

The gauge: Proposition 7.14

Write the three residuals in units of their \(\sigma\) as \(a, b, c\) (exact rationals). Proposition 7.14 splits them into an invariant line and a rotating plane,

$$s = a + b + c, \qquad z = a + \omega b + \omega^2 c = \Big(a - \frac{b + c}{2}\Big) + i\,\frac{\sqrt3}{2}\,(b - c),$$

and recovers \((a, b, c)\) exactly from \(s\) and \(z\) PROVEN. The page keeps \(z\) as the rational pair \((a - (b+c)/2,\ b - c)\), so the recovery is checked in BigInt on every update. The soliton core moves by \((z \text{ in the equatorial plane} + s \text{ along the axis})/3\), drawn through a saturating scale DEFINITION. Trefoil \(c\) shifts along its own 120° direction by its own residual, and the poles shift along the axis by \(s\). At lock every residual is zero, so the core sits exactly at the centre.

The soliton gauge, exactly

Two cases are worth trying. Equal residuals \((1,1,1)\) give \(z = 0\), so the core moves straight along the axis. Residuals that sum to zero give \(s = 0\), so it moves only in the plane.

The engine-frame loop, honestly

The step from each engine to the next is \(\sigma\), whose spinor is the Hurwitz unit \(\omega = (1 + i + j + k)/2\). The specification's fourth, \(H_4 = (H_1H_2H_3)^{-1}\), is then \(-1\), and the loop closes by construction. That is Lemma 12's cyclic convention: it tests nothing, so the page shows it as a parity element only.
